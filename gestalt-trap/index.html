The Gestalt Trap

A photomosaic engineered as three stacked attacks on how vision-language models actually process an image. A human sees a face in under a second. A VLM, forced into one fixed sampling of 16 px patches, sees a collage of clutter — and never feels that anything is missing.

Three layers, three failures

1 Macro — low spatial frequency

The portrait lives only in the per-tile average brightness — the lowest spatial frequencies in the image. Humans run parallel spatial-frequency channels and integrate globally at no cost. The model tokenizes into a fixed grid of 14–16 px patches at one effective resolution; the global low-frequency envelope is exactly what that pipeline discards.

2 Tiles — high-frequency clutter

Every tile is a coherent miniature scene — kitchens, street corners, machinery, harbors. Each keeps its own texture and color; only its mean luminance is shifted to match the face. So the patch-level statistics the embeddings are built from genuinely scream “cluttered collage of objects.”

3 Poison — typographic attack

Legible captions scattered in a few dozen tiles — the classic CLIP “iPod / apple” failure. Humans read them as decorative noise. The model treats text as ground truth and may answer with a caption it simply found inside the picture.

The testable prediction. A person glances and says “a face made of little pictures” in under a second. A VLM confidently describes a collage — or, worse, “an aerial view of a harbor at dusk.” And the failure is silent: a human illusion like the dress feels bistable, ambiguous. The model gets no phenomenal signal that it missed anything at all. It doesn’t even get to have the illusion.
